# Creative diversity doctrine

How Forge decides what a batch of ads should look like. Read this before
planning any batch.

## Why diversity is the lever now

Meta's ranking stack (the Andromeda retrieval upgrade) massively widened the
candidate pool it can consider and pushed personalisation down into retrieval.
The practical consequence for an advertiser:

**The creative IS the targeting.** You no longer hand Meta a narrow audience
and one polished ad. You hand it a broad audience and many genuinely different
ads, and the system finds which pocket of people each one belongs to.

This inverts the old instinct to perfect one execution. A batch of ten ads that
share a look gives the system **one** thing to work with. A batch of ten that
span different visual worlds gives it ten shots at finding an audience.

So: an ad that is merely a layout variant of another ad is not a second ad.

## The failure this exists to prevent

A six-ad Eden batch was planned as six distinct formats — notes app, struck job
post, comparison table, two-panel POV, editorial statement, search result. On
paper, six ideas. In the feed they were six variations of *dark background,
cream serif, minimal type*, because every one was built to the brand's visual
system.

They were format-diverse and **medium-identical**, which is the version that
does not help.

## Vary the MEDIUM, not just the layout

Before a batch ships, check that it spans several of these. Two ads in the same
row are near-duplicates no matter how different their copy is.

| medium | what it looks like |
|---|---|
| Native UI screenshot | iMessage, call log, calendar, inbox, notes, tweet, search result, CRM |
| Real photography | a person at a desk, a phone in a hand, an office, shot like a phone snap |
| UGC / selfie | face to camera, bad lighting, handheld, looks organic not produced |
| Meme / POV panels | two-panel POV, reaction format, deliberately internet-native |
| Editorial | magazine typography, generous space, premium and quiet |
| Infographic | comparison table, process diagram, checklist, before and after |
| Data visual | a chart, a number rendered large, a dashboard |
| Analog | handwriting, whiteboard, sticky note, printed document, receipt |
| Dramatic render | 3D, strong lighting, high contrast, product hero energy |
| Testimonial card | quote, stars, attribution, review-shaped |

## Brand consistency is subordinate to this

Follow the brand where it costs nothing. Break it where sameness is the bigger
risk. A UGC selfie ad should look like a real photo someone took, not like a
brand asset — putting the brand's serif and palette on it defeats the point of
running it.

The brand's job is the landing page and the product. An ad's job is to stop a
thumb.

## Diversity does not override the ICP test

Spanning mediums is how a batch gets reach. It is not how an individual ad gets
a click. Every ad still has to pass ICP-TEST.md from the buyer's chair, and a
medium nobody has used is not a reason to ship a concept the buyer would scroll
past. Reach for a new medium to carry a message that already works, not to
rescue one that does not.

## Practical rules for a batch

1. **No two ads in the same medium** until every medium has been used once.
2. **One angle per ad**, and no repeated angles inside a batch.
3. **Vary the emotional register**: some ads deadpan, some funny, some blunt,
   some aspirational. A batch that is all one tone is another kind of sameness.
4. **Keep what is proven.** Native UI screenshots are the strongest performing
   family on Eden's account. Diversity means adding new mediums, not discarding
   the winner.
5. **Ship the ugly one.** The best-performing ad in a batch is regularly the
   one that looks least like a designer made it. Do not filter those out for
   being off-brand.
