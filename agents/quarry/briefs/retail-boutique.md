# Retail & Boutique

Antique dealers, florists, tailors, record shops, cheesemongers, stationers.
Businesses where what's on the shelf is the argument, and the site's job is
to get out of its way.

## Palette

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Bottle | `#2B2E2A` | Text, nav, footer. A green-black, not a grey-black. |
| Leaf | `#F4F2F4` | Page ground. A paper white with a violet cast — deliberately not cream. |
| Plum | `#5B2A4A` | Headings, links. Sampled from anemone and dahlia, not picked from a swatch. |
| Stem | `#8FA37E` | Dividers, tags, quiet fills. |
| Petal | `#D4626A` | Single accent — CTA, sale flags. |
| Gilt | `#C9A227` | Antiques only. Hairline weight, never a fill. |

## Type

- **Display:** Fraunces, optical size turned up on large headings so the
  serifs get their proper flare. It has enough character to read as a shop
  with taste rather than a template.
- **Body:** Karla, 400/500. Slightly quirky grotesque that keeps the page
  from tipping fully period.
- Never set Fraunces below 20px. At small sizes it muddies and the page
  reads cheap — the exact opposite of what this category needs.

## Signature element: the specimen card

Products and services appear as specimen plates, the way a botanical guide
or an auction catalogue presents a lot. Each is: a generous image, a hairline
Stem box inset 8px from the image edge, and beneath it a small-caps caption
carrying a concrete label — a species and a stem count for a florist, a
period and a provenance for an antique dealer, a fabric and a turnaround for
a tailor.

**The colour must come from the goods.** For a florist, sample Petal from an
actual flower in the shop's own Google photos rather than using the hex
above verbatim — the palette here is a starting point, and a shop that sells
white peonies should not have a hot-pink button. This is the single rule
that stops every florist in the batch from looking like a sibling.

For antiques, Gilt only ever appears as a 1px rule or a letterform stroke.
The moment it fills a shape the page reads as a hotel brochure.

## Avoid

1. Cream ground, terracotta accent, large light-weight serif.
2. Near-black page, one neon accent, glassmorphic cards.
3. Broadsheet editorial — hairline boxes, tiny all-caps labels, giant
   centred display serif.

Also avoid: carousels, "Shop the look", full-width parallax, and any copy
that describes the business as "curated".

## Content rules

- If the business has real Google photos, the hero is one of them, full
  bleed, uncropped at the top. These shops are visual; a text hero wastes
  the only advantage they have.
- Instagram is often the real storefront for this category. If a handle is
  discoverable, link it in the nav at equal weight to the phone number.
- Hours above the fold, and say the neighbourhood by name — "Mile End",
  "NDG", "Petite-Italie". People find these shops by walking past them.
- **Quebec:** French is required under the Charter of the French language
  and must be at least as prominent as English. Build FR-first with an EN
  toggle; if only one language is generated, generate French.
